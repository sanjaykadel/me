How Far Does $1,000 to $1,500/Month Go in Nepal?

From high-speed fiber internet in Kathmandu to mountain cafes in Pokhara, explore how remote workers and digital nomads achieve upper-middle-class comfort in the Himalayas.

Nepal is famous around the world for its beautiful mountain peaks and fascinating cultural heritage, but for a remote worker, a digital nomad, or an expat, it offers another feature — an unbelievable opportunity to get an incredibly high purchasing power. Even when living on a small percentage of the average Western-style salary, an expat can enjoy modern life in Nepal. Spending $1,000 to $1,500 every month will allow you to reach the upper middle-class lifestyle in Nepal, enjoying modern comforts in a Himalaya country — from the best cafes in the valley to a private tour of the Everest region.

1. What $1,000 – $1,500 Can Get You

A local Nepalese person will manage quite well on $1500/month, but an expat can reach a really nice level of comfort. In Kathmandu (Jhamsikhel, Boudha, Lazimpat) or Pokhara (Lakeside), where most Westerners prefer to live because of the excellent infrastructure, you will be able to find:

  • Housing: Rent a 1-2 room modern unfurnished or fully furnished apartment without any issues (on average, $250-$500/month) with a constant backup power supply (inverter or solar panel), hot water, and high-speed fiber internet.
  • Food: Buy organic products in local markets and healthy food in cafes, eat at amazing local and international restaurants 4-5 times a week.
  • Internet: High-speed unlimited internet (fiber, 100-200 Mbps).
  • Transport: A local taxi or a ride from Pathao, InDrive, or other local apps.
  • Entertainment: A yoga studio or gym subscription, weekend hiking or road trip.
  • Electricity: 24/7 electricity supply (in most cases).
  • Water: Clean water (in most cases, but always check with locals if your apartment has an inverter, backup generator, and water filter).
  • Groceries: $300-$450/month for a small family of 2 people (1 adult + 1 child).

3. Best Places For Expats In Nepal

Kathmandu (Jhamsikhel & Sanepa)

Popular among expats, this place has many embassies, hostels, international cafes, and a high-speed internet connection.

Kathmandu (Boudha)

A great place for digital nomads, artists, and writers who want to enjoy the peace and explore the local Boudhanath Stupa.

Pokhara (Lakeside)

The silicon valley of Nepal — ideal for a digital nomad or someone who wants to enjoy the mountainous view over the Phewa Lake while having a cup of coffee in a local cafe. Compared to Kathmandu, prices for housing and food are 15–20% lower in Pokhara.

4. Important Notes For Expats In Nepal

Visa Notice: Currently, Nepal does not have a special Digital Nomad Visa, so most foreigners use a regular tourist visa or extend it through some business, education, or volunteering organizations.

Banks and Cards

The local market usually works with cash, so bring some Nepalese Rupees (NPR). Nevertheless, you can also pay with QR codes (Fonepay). We recommend having international cards (Wise, Charles Schwab, etc.) and withdrawing money from local ATMs in NPR to avoid currency conversion fees.

Power Supply

Although the whole country undergoes voltage fluctuations during the power supply, load shedding is now a relic of the past due to the abundance of hydropower stations. However, there may be blackouts during winter in certain areas, so it is important to clarify with the landlord whether your apartment has an inverter or another power backup.

Water Supply

In case of water scarcity in the city, which happens every 5-10 years, the water may be turned off for several days. Most houses in Kathmandu and Pokhara have filters or water storage tanks, but it is important to clarify this when renting an apartment.
